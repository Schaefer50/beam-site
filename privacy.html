Beam

Privacy Policy

Effective 6 October 2026

Beam is an app for Apple TV, iPhone and iPad. It plays media from servers and services you already use: Plex, Jellyfin, Emby and your own IPTV subscription. This policy explains what information Beam handles and where it goes.

In short

  • Beam has no account with us, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. We don't sell or share personal information.
  • Your server sign-ins stay on your devices and in your own iCloud account. We never receive them.
  • The only information that reaches a service we run is a Watch Together session, while you're using one (see below).
  • Diagnostics are sent only when you choose to send them, to whoever you choose.

Information stored on your device

To connect to your servers, Beam stores the following on the device:

  • The addresses you enter.
  • Sign-in tokens or passwords for Plex, Jellyfin, Emby, your IPTV provider, OpenSubtitles, Overseerr/Jellyseerr and Yahoo Fantasy.
  • Your settings: Live TV favourites, hidden categories, layout and playback preferences.

How sign-ins are kept:

  • They're kept in the device's Keychain.
  • Apple TV can clear an app's Keychain items when the app is updated. So Beam also keeps a copy in its own private storage on the device, where other apps can't read it.
  • Deleting Beam removes everything it stored on that device.

iCloud

If you're signed in to iCloud, the following sync to your other Apple TVs and iOS devices on the same Apple ID through your own iCloud account:

  • some settings;
  • your Live TV, Jellyfin/Emby and OpenSubtitles sign-ins.

This uses iCloud Keychain and iCloud key-value storage, so you don't have to sign in again on each device. A consequence is that signing out of one of those services on one device signs it out on all of them. We can't read your iCloud data. To turn this off, turn off iCloud for Beam in your device's settings.

Services you connect

When you browse or play, Beam talks directly from your device to the services you've connected:

  • your Plex servers and plex.tv (for Plex sign-in);
  • your Jellyfin or Emby servers;
  • your IPTV provider;
  • and, if you set them up, OpenSubtitles, Overseerr/Jellyseerr and Yahoo Fantasy.

What those services receive is covered by their own privacy policies. None of it passes through us.

Beam asks for local network access only to reach servers on your home network.

On iPhone and iPad it asks for the camera only to scan a Watch Together QR code. Nothing from the camera is stored or sent.

Watch Together

Watch Together keeps devices playing in sync. When you start a session, Beam creates a session record in a database we run on Google Firebase (Realtime Database). The record contains:

  • a six-character session code;
  • the display name of the person who started it;
  • the title being watched (its name and an identifier from your server);
  • the playback position and whether it's playing;
  • how many people have joined;
  • when the session was created;
  • a random identifier that Beam creates when it's installed, so devices in the session can tell each other apart. It isn't linked to your Apple ID or to any other app.

Anyone who has the session code can read that record, which is how joining works. The list of sessions can't be read.

How long it's kept:

  • The record is deleted when the host ends the session.
  • If the host's app closes before the session ends, the record can remain in the database. You can ask us to delete it, using the address below.

Watch Together is optional. If you don't use it, nothing is stored there.

Diagnostics and feedback

In Settings → About → Send Feedback, Beam can put together a diagnostics report. It does this only when you ask. The report contains:

  • Beam's version;
  • your device model and operating system version;
  • your time zone and region;
  • which kinds of server are connected, and how many libraries each has;
  • the playback settings that affect how files play;
  • Beam's own log lines from the current session.

Sign-in tokens and passwords are removed from those log lines before the report is made. A line can still include the network address of a server.

Beam doesn't send the report anywhere itself:

  • On iPhone and iPad you choose where it goes from the share sheet.
  • On Apple TV it's shown as a QR code for you to scan. The code opens our feedback page on your phone with the report inside the link itself, after the “#”. Browsers never send that part of a link to the website, so the report isn't uploaded to us; you choose whether to send it through TestFlight.

If you take part in a TestFlight beta, Apple may share crash reports and the feedback you submit with us, according to your TestFlight and device settings. Apple's privacy policy covers that.

Children

Beam isn't directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.
